About

I completed an M.Sc. in Bioinformatics at Banaras Hindu University, where I spent two years working with large-scale biological datasets, statistical models, and computational pipelines on national HPC infrastructure. That work taught me how to sit with messy, high-volume data until it tells you something true — a skill that transfers directly to business analytics.

I've applied that same rigor to two self-directed analytics projects covering HR attrition and customer segmentation — writing SQL to clean and aggregate data at scale, using Python to check what the data was really saying, and building Power BI dashboards that turn a finding into something a stakeholder could act on. I'm actively looking for my first Data Analyst role, where I can bring that same combination — research discipline plus business judgment — to real business problems.

Projects

HR Employee Attrition & Retention Analytics

Analyzed 1,470 employee records in PostgreSQL to find out why people were leaving — and what it was costing the business. Overtime turned out to be the strongest signal in the data: employees working overtime left at nearly three times the rate of everyone else. Built a Power BI dashboard so HR could track the risk by department, tenure, and overtime status without waiting on a new report each time.

30.5% vs 10.4% attrition, overtime vs not
$6.8M estimated annual cost
5 custom DAX measures
PostgreSQL Python Power BI

End-to-End E-Commerce Customer RFM Segmentation

Segmented 4,338 customers across 500K+ transactions using Recency, Frequency, and Monetary analysis in PostgreSQL. A small slice of the customer base — the "Champions" — turned out to drive more than half of all revenue, while over a third of customers had effectively gone quiet. Built an executive Power BI dashboard to make both groups easy to act on: who to retain, and who to win back.

58% of revenue from 18.1% of customers
